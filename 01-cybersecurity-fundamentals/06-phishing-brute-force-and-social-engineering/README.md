# Phishing, Brute Force and Social Engineering

## 📖 Overview

Phishing, Brute Force, and Social Engineering are common attack techniques used by cybercriminals to gain unauthorized access to systems, accounts, or sensitive information. These attacks often target people as well as technology, making user awareness an important part of cybersecurity.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, you should be able to:

- Understand phishing attacks.
- Understand brute force attacks.
- Understand social engineering.
- Identify common attack scenarios.
- Learn basic prevention techniques.

---

## 🎣 Phishing

Phishing is a cyber attack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.

### Common Targets

- Usernames
- Passwords
- Banking information
- OTPs
- Personal information

### Examples

- Fake bank emails
- Fake login pages
- Fake prize notifications
- Fake delivery messages

---

## 🔑 Brute Force Attack

A brute force attack is a method of guessing usernames or passwords by trying many possible combinations until the correct one is found.

### Common Characteristics

- Automated attack
- Targets weak passwords
- Can take seconds or months depending on password strength

### Prevention

- Strong passwords
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Account lockout policies
- Password managers

---

## 🧠 Social Engineering

Social Engineering is the manipulation of people to obtain confidential information or unauthorized access.

Instead of attacking systems directly, attackers exploit human trust.

### Common Techniques

- Pretending to be technical support
- Fake phone calls
- Fake emails
- Fake websites
- Urgent requests

---

## 🌍 Real-World Examples

### Example 1

An employee receives an email asking them to verify their company account by clicking a link.

This is a **Phishing** attack.

---

### Example 2

An attacker repeatedly attempts different password combinations until they successfully log in.

This is a **Brute Force** attack.

---

### Example 3

Someone calls an employee pretending to be from the IT department and asks for their password.

This is **Social Engineering**.

---

## 🛡️ Prevention

- Verify emails before clicking links.
- Use strong and unique passwords.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Never share passwords or OTPs.
- Be cautious of unexpected messages or calls.

---

## 💡 Key Points

- Phishing attempts to steal information through deception.
- Brute force attacks rely on repeated password guessing.
- Social engineering targets human behavior rather than technology.
- User awareness is one of the strongest defenses against these attacks.

---

## 📌 Summary

Phishing, Brute Force, and Social Engineering are widely used attack techniques that exploit either technical weaknesses or human trust. Understanding how these attacks work and following good security practices can significantly reduce the risk of becoming a victim.
